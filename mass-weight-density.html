⚖️ 1. Mass

What is Mass?

The mass of an object is a measure of the amount of matter it contains.

Mass is a basic property of a body. It is a constant quantity and does not depend on the location of the object.

The mass of an object also does not depend on its shape or on physical conditions such as temperature or pressure.

Key idea:

The mass of an object remains the same wherever the object is located.

Another Definition of Mass

Mass may also be defined as the amount of inertia an object possesses.

The greater the inertia of an object, the greater its mass.

📏 SI Unit of Mass

The SI unit of mass is the kilogram (kg).

Mass can be measured using a beam balance.

Example:

A student places an object on one side of a beam balance and adjusts standard masses on the other side until the balance is in equilibrium.

The total value of the standard masses gives the mass of the object.

🧠 Inertia

Inertia is the reluctance of an object to change its state of motion.

An object that is stationary tends to remain stationary. An object that is moving tends to continue moving with the same velocity unless a resultant force acts on it.

A force is required to overcome inertia.

The greater the mass of an object, the greater its inertia.

Therefore, the greater the mass, the more difficult it is to start the object moving or to stop it once it is moving.

🌍 Examples of Inertia

  • A loaded shopping trolley is harder to push from rest than an empty trolley.
  • A heavy truck is more difficult to stop than a bicycle travelling at the same speed.
  • A passenger may move forward when a moving bus suddenly stops.
  • A passenger may move backward when a stationary bus suddenly accelerates.

🌍 2. Weight and Gravitational Field

Gravitational Field

A gravitational field is a region in which a mass experiences a force due to gravitational attraction.

For example, the Earth produces a gravitational field around itself. Objects within this field experience a force of gravitational attraction towards the Earth.

Gravitational Field Strength

Gravitational field strength is defined as the force per unit mass.

g = F / m

where:

  • g = gravitational field strength
  • F = gravitational force
  • m = mass

Near the surface of the Earth, the gravitational field strength is approximately:

g ≈ 9.8 N/kg

Acceleration of Free Fall

The value of gravitational field strength is numerically the same as the acceleration of free fall near the surface of the Earth, when air resistance is ignored.

g ≈ 9.8 m/s²
Important:

9.8 N/kg and 9.8 m/s² have different units and describe related but different physical quantities.

9.8 N/kg → gravitational field strength
9.8 m/s² → acceleration of free fall

⚖️ Weight

The weight of a body is the pull of gravity on the body.

Weight is a force, so its unit is the newton (N).

Weight is a vector quantity because it has both magnitude and direction.

The direction of the weight of an object near the Earth's surface is vertically downwards, towards the centre of the Earth.

Weight can be measured using a spring balance.

⚖️ Relationship Between Mass and Weight

Weight is related to mass by:

W = mg

where:

  • W = weight in newtons (N)
  • m = mass in kilograms (kg)
  • g = gravitational field strength in N/kg

Worked Example

A student has a mass of 60 kg. Calculate the student's weight on Earth. Take g = 9.8 N/kg.

W = mg

W = 60 × 9.8

W = 588 N

🌍 Earth vs Moon

The mass of an object remains constant when it moves from Earth to the Moon.

However, its weight changes because the Moon has a different gravitational field strength.

🧊 3. Density

Definition of Density

The density of a substance is defined as the mass per unit volume.

ρ = m / V

where:

  • ρ = density
  • m = mass
  • V = volume

SI Unit of Density

The SI unit of mass is kg and the SI unit of volume is m³.

Therefore:

SI unit of density = kg/m³

Deduction of the SI Unit

Density = mass / volume

Unit of density = kg / m³

Therefore, SI unit = kg/m³

🔄 Rearranging the Density Equation

ρ = m/V

m = ρV

V = m/ρ

📏 Common Density Units

Density may also be expressed in:

  • g/cm³
  • g/mL
  • kg/m³
Useful conversion:

1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³

📝 Question 2

A concrete slab 1.0 m by 0.5 m by 0.1 m has a mass of 120 kg. What is the density of the concrete?

Volume:

V = l × w × h
V = 1.0 × 0.5 × 0.1
V = 0.05 m³

Density:
ρ = m/V
ρ = 120/0.05

ρ = 2400 kg/m³

📝 Question 3

An alloy is made by mixing 360 g of copper, of density 9 g/cm³, with 80 g of iron, of density 8 g/cm³. Find the density of the alloy, assuming the volume of each metal used does not change during mixing.

For copper:

Volume = mass / density
= 360/9
= 40 cm³

For iron:
Volume = 80/8
= 10 cm³

Total mass:
360 + 80 = 440 g

Total volume:
40 + 10 = 50 cm³

Density of alloy:
ρ = 440/50

ρ = 8.8 g/cm³

🔬 4. Determination of Density

🧊 A. Regular Solid — Example: Cuboid

A regular solid has a simple geometric shape, so its volume can be calculated using measurements.

Apparatus

  • Beam balance
  • Vernier calipers or suitable ruler

Procedure

  1. Determine the mass, m, of the rectangular glass block using a beam balance.
  2. Using a pair of vernier calipers, measure the height (h), width (w) and length (l) of the block.
  3. Calculate the volume using:
V = l × w × h

Calculate density using:

ρ = m/V

Precautions

  • Take care when reading the measuring instrument.
  • Check for zero error where applicable.
  • Take measurements at eye level to avoid parallax error.
  • Repeat measurements and calculate an average where appropriate.

🪨 B. Irregular Solid — Example: Stone

Apparatus

  • Beam balance
  • Measuring cylinder
  • Water

Procedure

  1. Measure the mass of the stone using a balance.
  2. Partially fill a measuring cylinder with water.
  3. Record the initial volume of water.
  4. Carefully place the stone into the water.
  5. Ensure the stone is completely submerged.
  6. Record the final volume.
  7. Calculate the volume of the stone.
Volume of stone = Final volume − Initial volume

Finally:

ρ = m/V
The stone should be completely submerged and should not trap air bubbles.

💧 C. Density of a Liquid

Procedure

  1. Measure the mass of an empty container.
  2. Pour a known volume of liquid into the container.
  3. Measure the mass of the container and liquid.
  4. Calculate the mass of the liquid by subtraction.
  5. Calculate density using ρ = m/V.
Mass of liquid = Mass of container + liquid − Mass of empty container

🪵 D. Irregular Solid That Floats — Example: Cork

A cork floats on water, so it cannot be measured using simple water displacement because it will not become fully submerged.

The cork must be forced completely below the water surface using a suitable sinker or another method.

Method Using a Sinker

  1. Measure the mass of the cork.
  2. Measure the volume of the sinker using water displacement.
  3. Attach the cork to the sinker.
  4. Fully submerge both objects in water.
  5. Measure the combined volume.
  6. Subtract the volume of the sinker.
  7. The remaining volume is the volume of the cork.
  8. Calculate the density of the cork using ρ = m/V.
Important: The object must be fully submerged when using the displacement method.

Question 5

An object has a mass of 500 g and a volume of 200 cm³. Calculate its density in g/cm³.

ρ = m/V

Question 6

m = ρV

m = 800 × 0.5

m = 400 kg

⚠️ Common Misconceptions

Incorrect Idea Correct Idea
Mass and weight are the same. Mass is the amount of matter; weight is gravitational force.
An object has less mass on the Moon. Mass stays constant; weight changes.
Heavy objects always fall faster. Ignoring air resistance, objects near Earth accelerate at approximately the same rate.
Density is the same as mass. Density is mass per unit volume.
A floating object cannot have its density measured. Its volume can be determined using a suitable displacement method.
