3. Parallax Error

Parallax error occurs when the position of an object or scale mark appears to change because it is viewed from different angles.

To avoid parallax error: The eye should be directly in line with the reading being taken. The line of sight should be perpendicular to the scale.
👁️ Correct and Incorrect Eye Positions
Question 2

Why should parallax error be avoided?

Parallax error can cause the scale reading to appear different depending on the observer's eye position, producing an inaccurate measurement.

4. Micrometer Screw Gauge

A micrometer screw gauge is suitable for measuring very small dimensions, such as the diameter of a thin wire or the thickness of a sheet of paper.

Precautions

  1. Check for zero error before taking measurements.
  2. Use the ratchet to apply controlled pressure.
  3. Do not overtighten the instrument.
  4. Take several readings at different positions where appropriate.
The ratchet prevents excessive force from being applied to the object, which could deform it and affect the measurement.
🔩 Parts of a Micrometer Screw Gauge

Reading a Micrometer

  1. Read the visible main scale on the sleeve.
  2. Read the thimble division aligned with the datum line.
  3. Add the two readings.
  4. Apply zero correction if necessary.
Measurement = Main scale reading + Thimble reading
Question 3

The main scale reads 5.00 mm and the thimble reads 0.32 mm. Find the measurement.

Measurement = 5.00 + 0.32

Answer = 5.32 mm

5. Volume

Volume is the amount of space occupied by an object or substance.

1 mL = 1 cm³

Measuring Liquid Volume

  • Place the measuring cylinder on a level surface.
  • Position your eye at the level of the liquid.
  • Read the bottom of the meniscus for water.
  • Record the reading with appropriate precision.
🧪 Reading a Measuring Cylinder

Read the bottom of the meniscus at eye level.

Volume of an Irregular Solid

The volume of an irregular solid that sinks in water can be found using the displacement method.

Volume = Final volume − Initial volume

The object must be completely submerged and air bubbles should be avoided.

Question 4

The initial water level is 40 cm³. After an irregular solid is completely submerged, the reading is 67 cm³. Find the volume of the solid.

Volume = 67 − 40

Answer = 27 cm³

6. Measuring Time

The SI unit of time is the second (s). Time intervals can be measured using clocks, stopwatches and digital timers.

For very short intervals, it is often more accurate to measure several repeated events and calculate the average time.

7. The Simple Pendulum

Important Terms

  • Oscillation: One complete cycle of motion.
  • Period (T): Time taken for one complete oscillation.
  • Frequency (f): Number of oscillations per second.
T = Total time ÷ Number of oscillations

f = 1 / T
🕰️ Simple Pendulum Animation

One complete oscillation is one complete cycle of the bob's motion.

Question 5

A student measures the time for 25 oscillations as 20.4 s. Calculate the period.

T = 20.4 ÷ 25

Answer = 0.816 s

9. Addition of Vectors

The resultant is a single vector that has the same overall effect as the vectors acting together.

Vectors in the Same Direction

Add their magnitudes.

Vectors in Opposite Directions

Subtract the smaller magnitude from the larger.

➡️ Vector Resultant

The resultant vector represents the combined effect of the two original vectors.

Perpendicular Vectors

When two vectors act at right angles, the magnitude of their resultant can be calculated using Pythagoras' theorem.

R = √(A² + B²)
Question 6

Two perpendicular forces of 7 N and 8 N act on an object. Calculate the magnitude of the resultant.

R = √(7² + 8²)

R = √113

Answer = 10.6 N

🏆 Final Challenge

You are given a thin wire and a micrometer screw gauge. Describe how you would obtain a reliable value for the diameter of the wire.

First check the micrometer for zero error. Measure the diameter at several different positions along the wire. At each position, use the ratchet to apply controlled pressure and avoid deforming the wire. Record all measurements and calculate a mean value. Taking several measurements reduces the effect of random errors and gives a more reliable estimate of the wire's diameter.
