1. Physical Quantities

A physical quantity is a property that can be measured and expressed using a numerical value and a unit.

For example, the length of a table might be 1.20 m. The number 1.20 is the numerical value and m is the unit.

Remember: A measurement should always include both a number and a unit.

2. SI Base Units

The International System of Units (SI) provides a standard system for measurements used throughout physics.

Quantity SI Unit Symbol
Length metre m
Mass kilogram kg
Time second s
Temperature kelvin K
Electric current ampere A
Question 1

Which SI unit is used to measure mass?

Answer: The SI unit of mass is the kilogram (kg).

Convert 3.5 cm into metres.

5. Parallax Error

Parallax error occurs when a scale is viewed from an angle rather than directly in line with the measurement.

To avoid parallax error, the eye should be positioned so that the line of sight is perpendicular to the scale at the point being read.
👁️ Correct Reading Position

Eye directly above the reading.

👁️

Eye viewing from an angle.

Question 3

Why should parallax error be avoided when taking a measurement?

Answer: Parallax error can cause the scale reading to appear different depending on the position of the observer's eye. This produces an incorrect measurement.

6. Zero Error

A measuring instrument has a zero error if it does not read zero when the true measured quantity is zero.

For example, a micrometer may show a small positive or negative reading when the anvil and spindle are touching.

Always check the zero position of an instrument before taking measurements.

If an instrument has a zero error, the measured value may need to be corrected.

7. Micrometer Screw Gauge

A micrometer screw gauge is used to measure very small dimensions, such as the diameter of a thin wire or the thickness of a sheet of paper.

A typical micrometer can measure to a precision of 0.01 mm.

How to use a micrometer

  1. Place the object between the anvil and spindle.
  2. Turn the ratchet to close the spindle gently.
  3. Stop tightening when the ratchet begins to click.
  4. Read the sleeve/main scale.
  5. Read the thimble scale.
  6. Add the two readings.
  7. Check and correct for any zero error.
🔩 Micrometer Screw Gauge

Important: Use the ratchet. Do not overtighten the micrometer, as excessive force may deform the object.

Question 4

Why should the ratchet be used when closing a micrometer?

Answer: The ratchet applies a controlled force. This prevents the object from being compressed or deformed and reduces measurement error.

8. Measuring Volume

Volume is the amount of space occupied by an object or substance.

Common units include:

  • cubic metre (m³)
  • cubic centimetre (cm³)
  • cubic millimetre (mm³)
  • litre (L)
  • millilitre (mL)
1 mL = 1 cm³

A measuring cylinder can be used to measure the volume of a liquid. The volume of an irregular solid can be found using water displacement.

🧪 Reading a Measuring Cylinder

Read the bottom of the meniscus at eye level for a liquid such as water.

Question 5

Why should a measuring cylinder be read at eye level?

Answer: Reading at eye level reduces parallax error and gives a more accurate measurement.

Volume of an Irregular Solid

If an irregular object sinks completely in water, its volume can be found by displacement.

Record the initial volume of water. Carefully place the object into the water and record the final volume.

Volume of object = Final volume − Initial volume

The object must be completely submerged and no air bubbles should be trapped on its surface.

Question 6

The initial volume of water is 40 cm³. After an irregular solid is fully submerged, the reading is 67 cm³. Find the volume of the solid.

Answer: Volume = Final volume − Initial volume

Volume = 67 − 40

Volume = 27 cm³

9. Measuring the Volume of a Floating Object

A floating object cannot simply be pushed below the surface and measured by ordinary displacement because it will rise back to the surface.

One suitable method is to attach a sinker of known volume to the object and submerge both completely.

Volume of floating object = Volume of object + sinker − Volume of sinker

The object must be completely submerged when the final reading is taken.

10. Measuring Time

The SI unit of time is the second (s).

Time intervals can be measured using clocks, watches, stopwatches and digital timers.

For short time intervals, repeated measurements can improve reliability.

Question 7

Why is it often better to measure the time for several repeated events rather than just one event?

Answer: The reaction time of the person starting and stopping the stopwatch has less effect on the total measurement when a longer time interval is measured. Dividing by the number of events gives a more reliable average value.

11. The Simple Pendulum

A simple pendulum consists of a bob suspended from a fixed point by a string.

One complete oscillation is one complete movement from one extreme position to the other extreme and back again.

The period (T) is the time taken for one complete oscillation.

The frequency (f) is the number of complete oscillations per second.

f = 1 / T
⏱️ Pendulum Animation

Measuring the Period

The time for one oscillation may be very short, making it difficult to measure accurately.

Instead, measure the time for a larger number of oscillations and divide by the number of oscillations.

T = Total time / Number of oscillations
Question 8

A student measures the time for 25 oscillations of a pendulum as 20.4 s. Calculate the period.

Answer: T = 20.4 / 25

T = 0.816 s

The period of the pendulum is 0.816 s.

12. Scalars and Vectors

A scalar quantity has magnitude only.

A vector quantity has both magnitude and direction.

Scalars Vectors
Distance Displacement
Speed Velocity
Time Acceleration
Mass Force
Energy Weight
Temperature Momentum
Electric field strength
Gravitational field strength
Question 9

Is velocity a scalar or a vector? Explain your answer.

Answer: Velocity is a vector because it has both magnitude and direction. For example, a velocity could be 20 m/s east.

13. Adding Vectors

When two or more vectors act together, they can be replaced by a single vector called the resultant.

The resultant has the same overall effect as the original vectors acting together.

For vectors acting in the same direction, their magnitudes are added.

For vectors acting in opposite directions, the smaller magnitude is subtracted from the larger magnitude.

Question 10

Two forces of 3 N and 5 N act in the same direction. Find the resultant force.

Answer: Resultant = 3 + 5

Resultant = 8 N

The resultant acts in the same direction as both forces.
Question 11

Two forces of 3 N and 5 N act in opposite directions. Find the resultant force and its direction.

Answer: Resultant = 5 − 3

Resultant = 2 N

The resultant acts in the direction of the 5 N force.

14. Resultant of Two Perpendicular Vectors

When two vectors act at right angles to each other, the resultant can be found using a scale diagram or Pythagoras' theorem.

R² = A² + B²

R = √(A² + B²)

Question 12

Two perpendicular forces of 7 N and 8 N act on an object. Calculate the magnitude of the resultant force.

Answer: R = √(7² + 8²)

R = √(49 + 64)

R = √113

R ≈ 10.6 N

15. Scale Diagrams

A vector can be represented by a line with an arrow. The length of the line represents the magnitude, while the arrow direction represents the direction of the vector.

For example, a scale of 1 cm = 1 N means that a force of 5 N is represented by a line 5 cm long.

Tip: Choose a scale that makes the diagram as large as reasonably possible. A larger diagram generally allows more accurate measurements.

Question 13

A student measures the diameter of a wire using a micrometer. The sleeve reading is 4.5 mm and the thimble reading is 0.08 mm. Calculate the diameter of the wire.

Answer: Diameter = Sleeve reading + Thimble reading

Diameter = 4.50 + 0.08

Diameter = 4.58 mm
Question 14

A student measures the time for 30 oscillations of a pendulum. The total time is 36.0 s. Calculate the frequency of the pendulum.

Answer: First calculate the period:

T = 36.0 / 30

T = 1.20 s

Now:

f = 1 / T

f = 1 / 1.20

f = 0.833 Hz
Question 15

Two perpendicular forces of 6 N and 8 N act on an object. Calculate the magnitude of the resultant.

Answer: R = √(6² + 8²)

R = √(36 + 64)

R = √100

R = 10 N
