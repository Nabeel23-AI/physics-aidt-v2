Choose an instrument according to the size of the object and the precision required.

Vernier calipersExternal diameter, internal diameter and depth
Micrometer screw gaugeVery small thicknesses and diameters

Parallax Error

Parallax error occurs when the scale is viewed from an angle. The apparent position of the mark changes with the observer's eye position.

To avoid parallax: Position your eye directly in line with the mark being read, so the line of sight is perpendicular to the scale.

Zero Error

An instrument has a zero error when it does not read zero when the true quantity being measured is zero. Check for zero error before taking measurements and apply the appropriate correction.

Question

Why should parallax error be avoided?

Parallax can make the scale appear to give different readings depending on the observer's eye position, producing an incorrect measurement.

3. Micrometer Screw Gauge

A micrometer screw gauge is used for very small measurements such as the diameter of a thin wire or the thickness of a sheet of paper.

Reading a micrometer

  1. Read the main/sleeve scale visible to the left of the thimble.
  2. Read the thimble scale where it aligns with the datum line.
  3. Multiply the thimble division by the least count.
  4. Add the readings.
  5. Apply any required zero correction.
Reading = Main scale reading + Thimble reading
Precaution: Use the ratchet to apply controlled pressure. Do not overtighten the spindle because the object may be deformed.

Worked Example

A micrometer has a main-scale reading of 5.00 mm and a thimble reading of 0.32 mm. Find the measurement.

Measurement = 5.00 + 0.32 = 5.32 mm.

4. Measuring Volume

Volume is the amount of space occupied by a substance or object.

1 mL = 1 cm³

Measuring liquids

Use a measuring cylinder. Place it on a level surface and read the liquid level at eye height. For water, read the bottom of the meniscus.

Volume by displacement

For an irregular solid that sinks, record the initial water volume, completely submerge the object, and record the final volume.

Volume of object = Final volume − Initial volume

Ensure the object is fully submerged and avoid trapped air bubbles.

Question

Water initially occupies 40 cm³. After an irregular solid is completely submerged, the reading is 67 cm³. Find the volume of the solid.

Volume = 67 − 40 = 27 cm³.

Floating objects

A floating object cannot simply be left floating and its displacement used directly. One method is to attach it to a sinker, ensure the combination is completely submerged, and use the displacement to determine the combined volume. Subtract the known volume of the sinker to obtain the object's volume.

5. Measuring Time & the Simple Pendulum

The SI unit of time is the second (s). For short intervals, repeated measurements can reduce the relative effect of stopwatch reaction time.

Key terms

  • Oscillation: one complete cycle of motion.
  • Period, T: time taken for one complete oscillation.
  • Frequency, f: number of oscillations per second, measured in hertz (Hz).
T = Total time / Number of oscillations     f = 1/T

⏱️ Pendulum Animation

Oscillations: 0

Press Start and observe one complete oscillation as the bob moves from one extreme to the other and back.

Question

A student measures 25 oscillations in 20.4 s. Calculate the period.

T = 20.4 ÷ 25 = 0.816 s.

Why measure many oscillations?

Timing many oscillations gives a longer total interval, so the stopwatch reaction-time uncertainty becomes a smaller fraction of the measurement. Dividing by the number of oscillations gives an average period.

6. Scalars & Vectors

A scalar has magnitude only. A vector has magnitude and direction.

ScalarsVectors
DistanceDisplacement
SpeedVelocity
TimeAcceleration
MassForce
EnergyWeight
TemperatureMomentum
Electric field strength
Gravitational field strength

Vector addition

Vectors can be represented by arrows. Arrow length represents magnitude and arrow direction represents direction. The single vector that has the same overall effect as the vectors acting together is the resultant.

For vectors in the same direction, magnitudes add. For opposite directions, subtract the smaller magnitude from the larger.

Perpendicular vectors

When two vectors act at 90°, the magnitude of the resultant can be found using Pythagoras' theorem.

R = √(A² + B²)

Question

Two perpendicular forces of 7 N and 8 N act on an object. Calculate the magnitude of the resultant.

R = √(7² + 8²) = √113 = 10.6 N (3 s.f.).

Scale diagrams

A scale diagram represents vectors using a chosen scale. For example, if 1 cm represents 1 N, a 7 N force is drawn as 7 cm. The resultant is then measured from the scale diagram.

🏆 Final Challenge

You have a thin wire, a micrometer screw gauge and a stopwatch. Explain how you would obtain a reliable value for the wire's diameter and reduce measurement uncertainty.

Use the micrometer and check for zero error first. Measure the diameter at several different positions along the wire. At each position, use the ratchet to apply controlled pressure and avoid deforming the wire. Record the readings and calculate a mean. Avoid parallax when reading the scale. Repeated measurements help reveal random variation and make the final value more reliable.
