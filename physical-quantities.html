📏 Measuring Length

Different instruments are used depending on the size and precision required.

  • Metre rule
  • Measuring tape
  • Vernier calipers
  • Micrometer screw gauge

Parallax Error

Parallax error occurs when an instrument is viewed from the wrong position. The apparent position of the scale reading changes because the eye is not directly above the measurement.

Remember: The line of sight should be perpendicular to the scale at the point where the reading is taken.

End Error / Zero Error

End error may occur when a ruler has a damaged end or when the zero mark does not lie at the edge of the ruler.

If the ruler is damaged at the zero end, place the object at another known mark. Read both ends of the object and subtract:

Length = Final reading − Initial reading

Example:

X = 1.0 cm
Y = 4.3 cm

Length = 4.3 − 1.0 = 3.3 cm

🔬 The Micrometer Screw Gauge

A micrometer screw gauge is used to measure very small lengths, such as the diameter of a fine wire or the thickness of paper.

A typical micrometer can measure to the nearest 0.01 mm.

Precautions

  1. Use the ratchet to close the micrometer.
  2. Do not overtighten.
  3. Check for zero error.
  4. Hold the object correctly between the anvil and spindle.
Why use the ratchet?
It prevents excessive force being applied to the object, which could deform it.

Reading a Micrometer

The reading consists of:

Total reading = Sleeve reading + Thimble reading
4.0 mm
0.08 mm

Total = 4.08 mm

🧪 Volume

Volume is the amount of space occupied by an object or substance.

Measuring Liquid Volume

A measuring cylinder can be used to measure the volume of a liquid. The reading should normally be taken at eye level, avoiding parallax error. For water and many other liquids, read the bottom of the meniscus.

Important: Always check the scale carefully and read the correct part of the meniscus.

Volume of an Irregular Solid

The volume of an irregular object can be found using water displacement.

Volume of object = Final water volume − Initial water volume

🧪 Displacement Challenge

Initial water volume = 45 cm³
Final water volume = 73 cm³

Objects That Float

If an object floats, it cannot simply be completely submerged and measured by displacement. A method must be used to force it below the surface, for example by attaching a sinking object whose volume is known.

⏱️ Time

The SI unit of time is the second (s). Other units include minutes, hours and days.

Time intervals can be measured using clocks, watches, stopwatches and digital timers.

The Simple Pendulum

One complete oscillation occurs when the bob moves from one extreme position to the other and back again.

  • Period (T): Time taken for one complete oscillation.
  • Frequency (f): Number of oscillations completed per second.
f = 1 / T

Measuring the Period

Measuring one oscillation directly may produce a large percentage uncertainty. Instead, measure the time for many oscillations and divide by the number of oscillations.

T = Total time / Number of oscillations

Resultant of Two Vectors

When two vectors act together, their combined effect can be represented by one vector called the resultant.

Same Direction

Resultant = A + B

Opposite Directions

Resultant = Larger force − Smaller force

For two forces of 2 N and 3 N:

Maximum resultant = 5 N
Minimum resultant = 1 N

Perpendicular Vectors

For two vectors at 90°, the magnitude of the resultant can be calculated using Pythagoras' theorem.

R² = A² + B²

🎮 Vector Resultant Challenge

Example

Find the resultant of 7 N and 8 N acting at 90°.

R = √(7² + 8²) = √113 ≈ 10.6 N
